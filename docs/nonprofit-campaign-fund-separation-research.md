# Nonprofit campaign advertising funds: separation and payment arrangements

Research date: 2026-11-09. Planning evidence for [Compare campaign fund separation and third-party payment arrangements](https://github.com/Cooper2Rich/adoption-project/issues/18), under [Plan Meta and TikTok nonprofit campaign accounts and funding](https://github.com/Cooper2Rich/adoption-project/issues/9). No vendor, rail, asset owner or live eligibility is selected. No setup, credentials, transfers, ads or implementation occurred.

## Target and conclusion

Latest user clarification governs: Slander manages **separate nonprofit organizations**; public advertising crowdfunding primarily finances each advertising fund, with optional nonprofit/client contributions. The requested provider holds a separate balance per **Slander campaign**. Slander creates and controls ads and automates payment where supported. adoption.is is first; the model must support later nonprofits. Earlier direct-client-billing preference is superseded. Nonprofit-owned financial or advertising assets can still be operated by an authorized Slander application; agency ownership of the payer is not inherently required.

**Finding:** provider-held balances and an ad-payment instrument are separate requirements. Stripe Connect alone supplies account balances, not an arbitrary Meta/TikTok spending wallet. Connect plus Issuing, or Treasury financial accounts plus cards, provides documented building blocks with important access and underwriting gates. Givebutter Wallet is a materially simpler nonprofit collection-and-spend candidate, but combines all campaigns in one organization account into one balance. None of these sources approves Slander's exact crowdfunding-to-advertising model or proves acceptance by both ad platforms. These are conditional operating paths, not verified end-to-end solutions.

## Terms and ownership boundaries

| Object | Meaning in this investigation |
| --- | --- |
| Nonprofit entity | The actual legal organization being verified and represented; not automatically Slander or the adoption.is brand. |
| Slander campaign | The application-level advertising effort and its advertising fund. One nonprofit may have several. |
| Platform campaign | Meta/TikTok advertising configuration, potentially several per Slander campaign. |
| Ad account | Platform advertiser/billing container; its ownership, permissions and invoice liability need a supported design. |
| Provider connected account | An onboarded entity's provider account with capabilities and contractual responsibilities; not a campaign tag. |
| Provider balance / financial account | Provider-maintained monetary record or financial product. Its agreements determine account holder and custody structure. |
| Payment instrument | A card, bank debit authorization or approved invoice-transfer route actually accepted by the advertising platform. |

Stripe's [Connect balance guide](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/account-balances) documents separate platform and connected-account pending/available balances. Charge placement determines where negative transactions first land; loss responsibility depends on account configuration and platform reserves can cover connected-account losses. **Inference:** account-level accounting separation is real provider tracking, but does not prove separate bank accounts, escrow, protection against all cross-account losses or campaign-level isolation inside one account.

For financial products, Stripe distinguishes direct bank DDAs (business is bank account holder) from FBO arrangements (bank holds pooled customer funds and Stripe records beneficial allocations). Labels alone do not establish legal rights or insurance. Review the actual agreements; do not apply the standalone Treasury structure automatically to a connected-account program. [Treasury account structures](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury). This note makes no insolvency, legal, tax-deductibility or insurance assurances.

## Documented provider gates

- Stripe classifies crowdfunding platforms and nonprofit fundraising as restricted activities requiring additional diligence; approval is service-specific and can change. Country-specific charitable-purpose restrictions also apply. Issuing prohibits consumer-funds uses and sets jurisdiction/integration requirements. Submit the actual business purpose—public contributions for nonprofit advertising, not personal donor wallets—and obtain the applicable product approvals. Nonprofit status alone is not approval. [Restricted businesses and Issuing prohibitions](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).
- [Direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted) are created on the connected nonprofit account; its balance increases. Application fees can separately accrue to the platform. **Inference:** this is a candidate for Slander-managed collection into nonprofit accounts, not a return to requiring clients to operate billing themselves.
- [Separate charges and transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers.md?platform=web&integration=checkout&ui=stripe-hosted) place charges, fees, refunds and chargebacks on the platform; transfers are separate and refunds do not automatically reverse them. Regions constrain transfers. Platform collection therefore changes responsibility, rather than merely changing the checkout branding.
- [Funds segregation](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/funds-segregation) is a private preview: allocated payment funds can only be transferred to connected accounts and are protected from unrelated platform debits. It is not an ad-spending balance. Preview access and platform negative-balance responsibility are prerequisites.
- [Manual payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/manual-payouts) can delay payout within country-specific holding periods. Stripe explicitly does not provide escrow. Ordinary payouts move money to the connected account's external account, not an advertiser ID. Holding periods must be checked after jurisdiction is known; no indefinite balance-holding promise is made.
- [Issuing with Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/connect) requires a business connected account and active Issuing capabilities; baseline documentation uses platform-controlled accounts with platform requirements collection and loss liability, with other configurations requiring access requests. Nonprofits are a documented business type, and need an authorized representative; applicable beneficial-owner information is conditional. This does not grant this nonprofit or Slander approval.
- The [connected-account Issuing funding guide](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/connect/funding) explicitly gates payments-balance-to-Issuing transfers behind the Balance Transfer API private beta. Bank pull funding is US-specific and requires verification and debit authorization; push funding availability varies. Remaining Issuing funds can be paid to the connected account's external bank. The general [Issuing balance guide](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/funding/balance) describes main-balance funding, but that does not override the Connect-specific beta gate.
- [Treasury for platforms](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/connect) requires sales access; current docs list GB public preview and other regions, including US, private preview. Do not confuse this with standalone Treasury availability. [Connected-account requirements](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/connect/account-management/connected-accounts) require a supported region, same-country representative, business review, capabilities, platform requirements collection and no Stripe-hosted dashboard.
- Treasury's [multiple-account example](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/connect/examples/multi-financial-accounts) creates two financial accounts for one connected business. [Funding documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/connect/moving-money/fund-a-financial-account) supports payments-balance funding and same-owner external-bank funding; it says external bank funding should use accounts owned by the account holder. **Inference:** collect public contributions through an approved payments flow, then route settled proceeds; do not expose a financial-account bank address as an assumed unrestricted public donation rail.
- [Issuing with financial accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/connect-v2) documents cards funded by financial accounts as an alternative to Issuing balances. Access, programs and capability configuration must match the approved Treasury/card design. [Outbound payments](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/connect/moving-money/out-of/outbound-payments) can pay third parties, but only prove a financial-provider capability, not acceptance or correct invoice referencing at Meta/TikTok.

## Comparison and operating paths

All flows below are **design inferences assembled from the documented capabilities above**. Feasible means a documented component path exists subject to the listed gates, not that onboarding or platform payment is approved.

| Path | What is externally separate? | Who is onboarded / pays? | Setup and ongoing burden | Fit to requested per-campaign balances |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. One platform payments balance + campaign ledger | Only Slander's provider balance; campaign labels are internal | Slander collection/payer entity; recipients also onboard if using Connect | Fewer primary accounts; most allocation and shortage reconciliation | Baseline comparison; does not meet provider-held separation by itself |
| B. Nonprofit Connect accounts + Issuing | Provider balance per nonprofit; separate Issuing balance, with cards | Each nonprofit plus Slander platform; nonprofit card account funds ads under delegated operation | Entity onboarding, card program, funding beta or bank round trip, API reconciliation | One campaign per nonprofit can align; several campaigns still share balances unless additional supported structure approved |
| C. Nonprofit Connect + campaign financial accounts + cards | Multiple provider financial-account balances under the nonprofit | Each nonprofit and its representative; Slander operates platform integration | Highest product-access and integration burden here; most direct documented multi-balance shape | Strong structural candidate, conditional on actual campaign-account/card linkage and approved program |
| D. Nonprofit bank account per campaign + instrument | Actual bank accounts in nonprofit's name, if bank approves; not simply nicknames | Each nonprofit/account signers; Slander needs delegated authority | Repeated bank onboarding/administration and provider-to-bank reconciliation | Can separate externally; changes provider-held requirement to bank-held funds |
| E. Givebutter nonprofit Wallet + spend card | Wallet per organization; all its campaigns pool | Each nonprofit/account representatives; authorized Admin/Finance operators | Hosted collection and card administration; fewer custom finance components | Candidate for one advertising campaign per organization; fails strict multi-campaign balance separation within one Wallet |

### A. Pooled platform collection and campaign bookkeeping

Collection -> Slander payment charge with durable campaign attribution -> wait for available funds -> approved payout to Slander's bank and accepted Slander instrument (or separately approved Issuing path) -> automatic ad charges -> reconcile receipts and platform campaign spend to ledger -> reserve for delayed bills/refunds -> distribute/refund remaining funds under chosen policy.

This is a reference path, not recommended as satisfying the user's provider-separation preference. A `transfer_group`, metadata or an app ledger associates records; it does not create a new account holder. Private-preview funds segregation can protect funds awaiting recipient transfer, but they cannot pay ads while allocated. If funds leave protected allocation into pooled spend, that protection does not follow merely because the ledger retains a label. Platform losses and shared-card charges expose pooled resources. An agency-owned card is a choice requiring approval, not a consequence of Slander running the app.

### B. Separately onboarded nonprofit accounts with Issuing

Collection -> approved direct charges on nonprofit connected account (or platform charges then reconciled transfers, with different loss responsibility) -> available nonprofit payments balance -> beta-approved transfer to its Issuing balance; **fallback conditional route:** payout to nonprofit bank, then authorized bank funding of that nonprofit Issuing balance -> campaign-designated card -> accepted recurring platform charges -> reconcile payment, transfer/payout, card authorization/capture and ad invoice -> retain reserves -> return unused Issuing funds through supported nonprofit-bank payout and fund contribution refunds from the appropriate collection balance.

Slander can automate supported API operations on behalf of the nonprofit after authorization. No evidence establishes that ordinary Connect transfer/payout directly buys ads. The bank fallback introduces settlement delays and is not an equivalent frictionless route. Multiple cards against one Issuing balance do not create separate campaign balances. Do not register a fictional campaign as a new nonprofit; provider approval is required for any repeated-account structure for one entity. A negative collection balance remains a different obligation even after money is moved to card spending.

### C. Multiple campaign financial accounts under each nonprofit

Collection -> nonprofit payments account -> available proceeds -> approved payout/funding to that campaign's financial account -> card drawing from the intended account, or approved third-party invoice payment -> ad charges -> reconcile financial account, card and advertiser invoice -> hold outstanding obligations -> remaining funds returned through supported same-owner flow; contribution refunds handled through their original collection objects with sufficient refund liquidity.

This is the clearest documented candidate for provider-maintained balances for **several campaigns of one nonprofit**. Multiple financial accounts remain under that entity; they do not become separate legal nonprofits. Require confirmation that the approved card program can draw each card from the designated campaign account, which transfers can cross accounts, how deficits/reserves are recovered, and the contractual DDA/FBO custody arrangement. Multiple-account examples for restaurants are capability evidence, not nonprofit-crowdfunding acceptance. Automatic transfers or invoice payments must target an approved payee and billing reference; generic outbound-payment support is insufficient.

### D. Bank-held campaign accounts

Collection on approved nonprofit provider account -> available funds -> payout to verified nonprofit campaign bank account -> account-linked debit card or approved bank/invoice rail -> platform charges -> reconcile provider payout, bank statement and ad invoice -> reserve liabilities -> retain, return or refund according to approved policy. A credit card instead creates an issuer credit liability with later bank repayment; it is not a provider-held campaign balance.

Concrete US bank evidence exists: Chase documents [nonprofit banking](https://www.chase.com/business/banking/nonprofit), [entity and signer verification](https://www.chase.com/business/resources/business-bank-account-information), and [business debit cards for account owners/signers](https://www.chase.com/content/dam/chase-ux/documents/personal/checking/biz-how-your-transaction-will-work.pdf). This is an example of bank capabilities, not a bank selection or approval of campaign-specific accounts/Slander access. Ask the bank to confirm separate account titles/numbers, signer authority, campaign purpose, overdraft and offset terms, card linkage and automation. A savings bucket or virtual account number alone need not be a separate deposit account. Separate nonprofit entities require their own verification; several campaign bank accounts for one nonprofit still share the same entity owner. This path only fits if the human accepts bank-held funds as a change to the requested provider-held model.

### E. Simpler hosted nonprofit collection and Wallet spending

Givebutter [Wallet documentation](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/12295008-how-to-use-givebutter-wallet) states that settled campaign transactions can flow daily into one Wallet balance, with linked-bank deposits also supported. Funds are held at Fifth Third Bank through Stripe account services; Visa commercial cards are issued by Celtic Bank. It explicitly pools **all campaigns**, preserving campaign reports rather than separate Wallet balances.

Collection on the nonprofit's hosted campaign -> settlement and payout approval -> daily Wallet transfer -> accepted spend-card charges -> reconcile payout detail, Wallet/card activity and platform invoice -> maintain refund liquidity and outstanding-bill reserves -> withdraw remaining funds to linked nonprofit bank or execute original-transaction refunds from the required collection balance. Optional client contributions must use a supported contribution or same-owner funding route, not an assumed arbitrary third-party bank deposit.

[Spend cards](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/12821739-how-to-create-and-use-spend-cards) draw available Wallet funds and support virtual cards and spending policies. Admin/Finance users currently issue cards for themselves, and these roles can manage all account cards. Freezing blocks new purchases but does not prevent previously authorized transactions completing. **Inference:** campaign-specific cards improve attribution but do not isolate Wallet funds. Confirm that Slander personnel can hold the intended delegated role/card and that actual Meta/TikTok recurring charges accept the instrument. No supported programmatic card-creation/advertiser-payment API was established in these pages; hosted UI setup followed by merchant automatic billing is a different automation boundary from Slander controlling every financial action through an API.

Givebutter [country requirements](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/1726542-supported-countries-and-currencies-on-givebutter) require US bank/presence details and process donations in USD; [Wallet product description](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/1726586-what-is-givebutter-and-how-does-it-work) identifies US/Puerto Rico nonprofits and registered businesses as eligible categories. Actual approval remains unknown. Separate organization accounts align with separate nonprofits; do not assume duplicate Wallet accounts per campaign of one nonprofit are permitted. [Withdrawals/disputes](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/1761146-how-to-withdraw-funds) are subject to availability/review, and disputes can create negative balances and block payouts. [Refund instructions](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/7109570-how-to-issue-a-refund) explicitly require Payouts available balance: Wallet funds cannot directly issue contribution refunds. This is a significant ongoing reserve/replenishment requirement.

## Shared versus separate ad accounts: financial consequences

This comparison is architectural inference; platform eligibility is a separate investigation. An ad account is not a provider account. No inspected source proves assignment of a different billing card to each platform campaign within a shared ad account.

| Advertising structure | Financial operation | Error containment and workload |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One shared account per platform, many nonprofits | One billing context may cover several campaign expenses; allocation needs platform spend/invoice records and an explicitly approved payer arrangement | Fewer billing setups, but merchant charges need not identify the nonprofit or Slander campaign. A card issuer seeing the same merchant cannot reliably distinguish two platform campaigns. Multiple provider balances do not solve that mismatch. |
| Separate advertiser account per Slander campaign | Seek approved one-to-one mapping from campaign financial balance -> instrument -> advertiser billing context | More setup, payment verification, invoices and account administration. Better charge attribution and containment if instrument and funds are genuinely separate. A shared fallback card or pooled reserve can reconnect the risks. |
| Separate account per nonprofit, multiple campaigns within it | Separates entities' advertiser billing but still requires campaign allocation within each nonprofit | Useful intermediate model if platform policy and human ownership choice permit; does not fulfill strict per-campaign isolation alone. |

Slander can manage nonprofit-owned assets under supported grants. The billing entity, instrument holder and payer authority must be agreed independently from app operator and ad-account owner. Account counts are not a substitute for platform approval or permitted billing design.

TikTok's current [automatic-payment guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/automatic-payment?lang=en) bills at threshold or bill date and warns failed methods can require manual payment. This establishes merchant-initiated billing, not approval of any candidate card. Meta's [accepted-payment-method page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/212763688755026) returned a login/temporary-block page in this investigation: **inaccessible, not substantive verification**. The earlier [funding research](https://github.com/Cooper2Rich/adoption-project/blob/research/meta-tiktok-advertising-funding/docs/meta-tiktok-advertising-funding-research.md) and [access research](https://github.com/Cooper2Rich/adoption-project/blob/research/meta-tiktok-advertising-access/docs/meta-tiktok-advertising-integration-research.md) are context, not approval for the revised model.

## Failure containment and reconciliation design conditions

The following are proposed operating requirements, not provider guarantees:

- Wrong campaign: bind nonprofit, Slander campaign, provider account/financial-account ID, instrument ID, platform/ad-account ID and currency before spend. Quarantine mismatches; do not repair solely by relabeling another nonprofit's loss. Shared-account bills require explicit allocation evidence and correction policy.
- Unsettled contributions: public amount raised, successful collection, available payments balance, transferred card balance, reserved authorizations and settled ad charges are different states. Spend only cleared, unreserved funding; preserve an agreed reserve for reversals. A later dispute can still arrive after an ad ran.
- Card controls: Stripe [spending controls](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/controls/spending-controls) support categories, countries and limits, with merchant-ID controls in private preview. Aggregation is best effort and later fees/captures can exceed a limit. **Inference:** controls reduce errors but are not guaranteed campaign escrow or exact maximum ad cost; merchant category restrictions cannot distinguish campaigns buying from the same platform.
- Shared instruments: a dedicated card number helps trace a charge only if used exclusively in its mapped advertiser account. A single card across campaigns makes financial isolation depend on after-the-fact allocation; changing the default card is not evidence old liabilities disappear. Record actual charged instrument and account, including retries and fallback methods.
- Declines/overspend: stopping a card does not undo ad services already delivered. Confirm who owes unpaid platform charges and which provider/platform party covers collection disputes. Pause advertising before available reserves are exhausted; treat availability polling and platform reporting as potentially delayed.
- Reconciliation chain: contribution/payment ID -> campaign/beneficiary -> fees and availability -> transfer/payout/funding ID -> source and destination balances -> authorization/capture/card ID -> platform invoice/payment ID -> attributable platform campaign spend. Track pending, failed, returned and duplicate events separately. Merchant credits and donor refunds are distinct transactions.
- Closure: stop new ad delivery and contributions as agreed; reconcile delayed platform invoices, card captures and credits; keep required dispute/refund reserves and authorized access; resolve deficits; then return or retain the residual under an explicit human-approved purpose/refund policy. No automatic reuse for another nonprofit is inferred.

## Conditional recommendations and unresolved checks

1. If strict provider-held balances **per campaign**, including several campaigns per nonprofit, remain mandatory: investigate path C first for fit, not vendor selection. Obtain written approval for nonprofit crowdfunding for advertising, multiple financial accounts, each card's funding linkage, country/entity capability and custody/loss terms. Public docs establish components, not approval.
2. If adoption.is initially means one advertising campaign for one eligible US nonprofit and hosted finance administration is acceptable: path E may avoid building an embedded-finance integration. It fails the reusable strict multi-campaign separation requirement unless the provider supplies a separately approved arrangement. Confirm delegated Slander operation, actual ad-card acceptance and refund replenishment first.
3. If each nonprofit has only one campaign and Stripe grants the specific funding beta/card program: path B may suffice. Multiple cards in one balance do not extend that conclusion to several campaigns. Bank round-trip funding trades private-beta dependency for additional operational steps.
4. If bank custody satisfies the human's objective: path D is a distinct fallback with bank-approved entity accounts and authority. Path A is a comparator only; labels in a shared balance do not meet the stated preference.

Before choosing any vendor/rail, establish: Slander's and each nonprofit's legal identity/country/status; the legal recipient and collection merchant; donor advertising-purpose terms and residual/refund rules; platform bill-to party, account owner and spending authorization; account-specific crowdfunding/Connect/Treasury/Issuing approvals; supported currencies and cross-border flows; cardholder and instrument acceptance at both platforms; funds availability, reserves, negative-balance recovery/offset and delayed charges; practical API versus hosted-UI automation boundaries; full reconciliation and failure/closure behavior. Eligibility cannot be settled with missing entity/country details and public documentation alone.
