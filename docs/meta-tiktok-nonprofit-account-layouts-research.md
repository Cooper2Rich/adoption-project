# Meta and TikTok account layouts for Slander-managed nonprofits

Evidence checked 2026-11-09. Planning only. Research for [Compare shared and per-campaign Meta and TikTok ad accounts](https://github.com/Cooper2Rich/adoption-project/issues/17), within [Plan Meta and TikTok nonprofit campaign accounts and funding](https://github.com/Cooper2Rich/adoption-project/issues/9). Assignment to Cooper2Rich verified before investigation. No account setup, credentials, payments, ads or implementation performed. Disposable research branch; no PR; never merge.

## Scope and result

The latest user clarification governs: **separate nonprofit organizations** managed by Slander; public advertising crowdfunding is the primary advertising-fund source, supplemented by nonprofit/client contributions; a payment provider should hold a separate balance per Slander campaign; Slander creates/controls ads and automatically pays platforms if supported. Earlier direct-client billing and presumed client asset ownership are superseded or reopened. Adoption.is is first, with reusable later nonprofit campaigns. This note does not choose asset ownership or a payment provider.

**Conditional recommendation (inference): begin planning around one advertiser account per nonprofit per platform, with multiple platform campaigns for that nonprofit's Slander campaigns.** Choose per-Slander-campaign advertiser accounts when an approved payment rail or separate account permissions/caps require that boundary and account quotas permit it. Do not select one cross-nonprofit advertiser account merely because it reduces setup. Current readable evidence does not approve that design, and Meta's controlling account-structure material could not be read. TikTok explicitly describes client-specific agency creation, not an unrestricted multi-client advertiser identity. Neither layout proves automated crowdfunding settlement or campaign-fund segregation.

A critical upstream gate: TikTok's [NGO/NPO advertising policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/non-governmental-organization-and-non-profit-organization?lang=en), updated September 2026 on direct inspection, allows nonprofit advertising only in limited circumstances through approval and requires work with a TikTok Sales Representative. Eligibility varies by registration market; the market-specific panel was not exposed in readable content. Adoption.is's legal entity, intended markets and actual fundraising/ad creatives need approval. Slander's agency status does not establish an exemption. No layout is launch-ready until this is resolved.

Evidence labels: **D** = readable current official documentation; **S** = official generated SDK/schema, proves a surface rather than permission/eligibility; **U** = inaccessible or account-specific unknown; **I** = engineering inference/recommendation. Search snippets and secondary summaries were discovery aids, not proof of current rules. All exact source URLs below are primary except prior repository research, which is context only.

## Distinct objects

| Object | Meaning and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Nonprofit entity | The actual legal organization promoted; Slander managing it does not make it part of Slander. Confirm adoption.is's actual legal entity rather than treating its domain as one. |
| Slander campaign | Internal fundraising/creative activity with its own advertising fund and contribution rules. Several can belong to one nonprofit. |
| Platform campaign | Meta campaign with ad sets/ads, or TikTok campaign with ad groups/ads. One Slander campaign can map to several platform campaigns. |
| Business workspace | Meta business portfolio or TikTok Business Center (BC), owning assets or receiving partner access. A shared workspace is not a shared advertiser account. |
| Advertiser/ad account | Container for campaigns, billing configuration, currency/timezone, access and reporting. Owner, end advertiser and payer can be different roles requiring truthful declarations and approval. |
| Brand/ad identity | Facebook Page/Instagram identity or TikTok account/post authorization. Access to an advertiser account alone does not establish every identity right. |
| Provider account/balance | External collection-provider arrangement requested per Slander campaign. Its custody, legal separation, fees, reserves and withdrawal rails remain separate research. |
| Platform balance/credit | Money or credit recognized inside Meta/TikTok; not the collection-provider balance. |
| Payment instrument | A particular approved card, debit mandate, invoice rail or credit arrangement; not an ad ID, campaign ID or internal ledger. |

The glossary's **advertising fund** remains distinct from fundraising donations, creator earnings and the prize fund. Local AGENTS.md, issue-tracker/domain instructions and GLOSSARY.md were read. ## Platform rules and evidence boundaries

### TikTok: supported agency/account structure

**D:** [Create ad accounts in BC](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/create-ad-accounts-in-business-center) (March 2026) distinguishes advertisers promoting themselves from agencies promoting clients. Agency creation takes the client's company details, account details and billing information; BC owns created accounts. An admin uses Accounts → Advertiser accounts → Add advertiser account → Create New. Exact client company identification matters; company-name changes trigger review. After a client's initial approval, its verified legal-entity certificate can be reused for further accounts. Creation quotas vary by BC type; ask support/representative for the actual quota. Creator Agency BCs cannot create these accounts. This supports multiple accounts for one client when eligible, without inventing a universal account count.

**D:** [Request advertiser access](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/request-access-to-ad-accounts-in-business-center?lang=en) (March 2026) provides the alternative: BC admin requests access by advertiser ID, requests Admin/Operator/Analyst permissions, declares primary-media-agency status where applicable, and the account owner approves. This is delegated management, not ownership transfer. Disapproved accounts cannot be requested. Its Operator description includes financial functionality, so do not assume an operator is inherently barred from finances; verify actual roles and grants.

**I/U:** These documents establish two real multi-client management paths: Slander-owned client-specific advertisers, or nonprofit-owned advertisers shared with Slander. They do not establish permission to declare one advertiser as Slander and quietly rotate unrelated nonprofits underneath it. Multiple linked TikTok identities do not resolve advertiser-company verification. Obtain representative confirmation before considering a shared cross-entity advertiser. No explicit universal TikTok prohibition on every possible multi-brand shared advertiser arrangement was established in the reviewed primary pages.

### Meta: current policy verification remains incomplete

**U:** The [Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/) returned HTTP 429. [Developer Policies](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/development/terms-and-policies/platform-policy/) was inaccessible. The April 28, 2026 official [account-structure/transparency announcement](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2026/04/28/updating-our-transparency-requirements-for-ad-buying-solutions/) also returned HTTP 429. Secondary search results point to separate-end-advertiser requirements and a possible catalog/Pixel/CAPI signal exception, but **neither the current rule wording nor exception eligibility is verified here**. Do not assert a blanket exception, assume a vendor/brand field qualifies nonprofit fundraising, or assert a universal ban without obtaining readable primary text or written Meta approval. Shared cross-nonprofit Meta advertising remains unsupported by this investigation, not proven impossible.

**U:** Official [adding accounts](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/915885887059947) and [related ownership guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1717412048538897) returned login/temporary-block pages. Current creation quota, whether closed accounts retain quota, create-versus-claim-versus-request-access rules, ownership transfer restrictions, full-control/finance tasks and exact UI steps need current official/account confirmation. No universal numerical limit or quota-reset claim is certified.

**S:** Meta's [Business SDK object](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebook/facebook-python-business-sdk/main/facebook_business/adobjects/business.py) exposes distinct owned/client account surfaces and `POST /{business-id}/adaccount` with end-advertiser, media-agency, partner, currency, timezone and funding parameters. `create_owned_ad_account` instead takes an existing account ID; it is not interchangeable with provisioning. These are schema facts, not an assurance Slander may create unlimited advertisers, transfer ownership or attach arbitrary funding sources.

**S:** [AdAccount schema](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebook/facebook-python-business-sdk/main/facebook_business/adobjects/adaccount.py) separates owner/end-advertiser/media agency, funding source, currency/timezone, spend cap and account status, and exposes Insights filtering/reporting. [Creative schema](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebook/facebook-python-business-sdk/main/facebook_business/adobjects/adcreative.py) separates story, Instagram identity and creative references. **I:** preserve these distinctions; do not overwrite the end advertiser each time Slander rotates clients.

**D:** Meta's own [Marketing API collection](https://www.postman.com/meta/facebook-marketing-api/collection/0zr4mes/facebook-marketing-api-mapi) separates app, account, token and permissions and identifies Advanced Access for managing other people's accounts. **U:** exact current business verification, app-review/access assessment and Page/Instagram tasks for the selected creative/placement remain gates. Agency ownership is not assumed to bypass third-party advertiser obligations. The collection's generalized token-lifetime text is not used as a lifecycle contract.

## Identities, access and automation

**D:** TikTok's [Custom Identity transition](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-changes-coming-to-custom-identity?lang=en) (March 2026) phases out invented profile images/names for ordinary new campaigns from January 2026, with enumerated campaign exceptions. It supports multiple TikTok identities/advertisers through BC and warns that direct Ads Manager linking is individual-user access. Plan ordinary nonprofit ads around authorized real nonprofit identities rather than assuming an exception.

**D:** [Ad-delivery permission request](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-request-tiktok-account-ad-delivery-permissions-in-business-center) distinguishes post use, publishing and ads-only scope; account-user authorization is part of linking. [Link identity to advertiser](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-link-a-tiktok-account-for-ad-delivery-to-ad-accounts-in-tiktok?lang=en) (October 2026) requires admin access and warns that verification restrictions can apply. Once linked, members with advertiser access can use the identity for delivery. **I:** linking all nonprofits to one advertiser broadens identity exposure; account separation only helps if identities and members are correctly scoped.

**D:** [BC roles](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-business-center-roles-and-permissions?lang=en) distinguishes basic roles from Finance Manager/Analyst and says admins need advanced finance roles for finance management. API scopes remain separate. **I:** assign campaign operators, reporting access and finance duties deliberately; scope automation principals to needed accounts/assets. A Slander-wide superuser retains broad error exposure under every layout.

**S:** TikTok's [official SDK endpoint inventory](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tiktok/tiktok-business-api-sdk/main/java_sdk/README.md) describes campaign/ad-group/ad creation, status updates and reports. This supports automation candidates, not the current production contract or account eligibility. Together with Meta's schemas, it supports later automation of discovery, paused publishing, status/budget changes and reporting after access is approved. UI account creation, nonprofit approval, legal-entity verification, identity consent, payment-method verification and app review must not be promised as unattended provisioning. Exact TikTok advertiser-creation API eligibility was not established; a guessed AdvertiserApi.md URL returned 404 and supplies no evidence of API absence.

## Three layouts: setup, operation and closure

All paths first need truthful entity/owner/payer decisions, nonprofit advertising eligibility, supported markets/currency/timezone, asset inventory and permissions, production app approval, finance authority and an approved funding rail. These are future steps, not actions performed here. Meta setup sequences below are proposed checklists pending the inaccessible current help; TikTok agency/request sequences above are documented.

| Layout | Setup and ongoing operation (I unless documented above) | Closure and main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. One shared advertiser per platform for all nonprofits | Obtain explicit platform support for this cross-entity arrangement first; establish advertiser/beneficiary/payer disclosures. Create/authorize one advertiser, link each permitted brand identity, and map every Slander campaign to disjoint platform campaigns. Apply campaign/ad-group/ad-set budgets; perform all reporting and charge allocation using object IDs. Account-level funding/instruments must be reconciled across separate external campaign balances. | Pause only the closing campaign's objects; preserve account/other campaigns. Reconcile its later charges and reserve obligations before external balance release. Fewest account onboarding events, but all campaigns share account access, billing interruption and account-cap effects. Not an approved baseline. |
| B. Advertiser per Slander campaign | For every new fundraising campaign, create one advertiser per platform identifying its actual nonprofit (not a fictitious new company), or authorize a properly owned existing dedicated account. Repeat billing/currency/timezone, grants, brand linking, cap and reporting setup. Reuse legitimate entity verification where supported. Map this account exclusively to that Slander campaign and separately map its platform campaigns/entries. | Pause all campaign accounts' objects, settle/reconcile, retain history, then close or retain dormant accounts according to provider rules. Strongest alignment of campaign, account cap and potential instrument; greatest quota/onboarding/verification burden and more IDs to misroute. Ownership transfer and refunds are not automatically easy. |
| C. Advertiser per nonprofit, multiple Slander campaigns within it | Establish client-specific accounts and grants once per nonprofit/platform. For each later Slander campaign reuse the nonprofit's verified identity and authorized advertiser, create disjoint platform campaigns with campaign-level controls, and retain a separate external advertising-fund balance and allocation ledger. | Close/pause the Slander campaign's platform objects while retaining the nonprofit account and history. Account closure is only appropriate when the nonprofit stops using it. Less repeated setup than B; sibling campaigns share account permissions, billing and account cap. External per-campaign balances do not become platform sub-balances. |

For **each** row, ownership has two alternatives: nonprofit-owned account shared to Slander, or Slander-owned client-specific account with truthful nonprofit advertiser declaration and brand-asset grants. The former may simplify eventual agency replacement, but Slander-funded automated payment is still unverified. The latter aligns with TikTok's documented agency creation route, but creates an asset exit obligation and does not approve cross-entity cash pooling. Owner choice must cover ad accounts, Page/Instagram/TikTok identities, pixels/datasets, audiences, developer app and billing liability separately. No recommendation silently requires transfer of the nonprofit's existing assets.

### Common operating sequence (I)

1. Bind nonprofit ID → Slander campaign ID → platform business/advertiser ID → allowed identities/destinations/pixels → platform campaign/ad-set or group/ad IDs → external provider balance and approved payment rail. Use immutable IDs and effective dates, not names or account-switcher defaults.
2. Before writes, validate grant, account/identity status, selected owner/end advertiser/payer, spend authority, currency/timezone and that every parent object belongs to the expected account. Create paused objects, read back the mapping and configuration, and enable only under the later selected approval policy.
3. Reconcile desired and effective delivery state; monitor review failures, permissions, payment failure and actual accrued spend. Do not blindly retry an uncertain successful creation or budget increment. Preserve external result IDs for investigation.
4. Collect ad-level performance with account/object identifiers, account date/timezone, currency and attribution settings. Join competition entries and tracked fundraising links explicitly. Platform attributed conversions, confirmed fundraising and cash settlement are different records. Cross-account reports need pagination, completeness checks and normalization; account totals alone cannot divide a shared advertiser among campaigns.
5. End delivery, confirm effective pauses, cancel queued enables, ingest late/corrected spend, settle outstanding invoices, reconcile credits/refunds, then revoke only no-longer-needed grants. Revocation alone is not evidence ads have stopped. Retain historical reports, IDs and financial reconciliation after disconnect.

## Funding topology and spend containment

**D:** [TikTok automatic payment](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/set-up-automatic-payment) bills at threshold/date using a valid method; BC-owned accounts add methods in BC; prepaid cards are excluded and availability varies. This documents platform-initiated automation, not permission to debit an arbitrary collection-provider balance. Do not assume a provider-issued virtual card is accepted merely because it has card details.

**D:** [Payment Portfolio](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-payment-portfolio?lang=en) supports advanced cash/credit sharing among accounts under the same entity name and standard manual allocation; multiple portfolios can separate methods/balances within an entity. It describes sharing as suited to cases without multi-party billing. [Automatic Balance Allocation](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-auto-allocate-balance-in-business-center?redirected=2) describes owned, monthly-invoiced accounts drawing from shared BC funds and an irreversible upgrade. Neither proves shared funding across independent nonprofits or automatic external settlement from the requested provider.

**I:** In A, approved pooled platform billing requires allocation of aggregate charges among external campaign balances. In C the same problem remains among one nonprofit's sibling campaigns. Choosing different payment instruments inside one account cannot be assumed to route charges by platform campaign. In B, one approved instrument/funding allocation per account could align charges with one external balance, but only if both providers support and approve the complete rail. One shared credit line/card/BC balance can financially reconnect otherwise separate accounts. No layout constitutes bank segregation or guarantees ban/financial isolation.

**D:** [TikTok Budget Manager](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-use-budget-manager) documents daily, monthly and one-time account caps that pause ads when reached; Unlimited is also possible. It is an account-wide control, not a campaign-specific wallet. [Daily budget guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-set-up-a-daily-budget-in-tiktok-ads-manager?lang=en) describes an average/dynamic budget with higher daily spending possible. A daily budget is not a same-day cash ceiling. Meta's schema exposes `spend_cap`; **U:** its exact current eligibility, reset semantics, interaction with billing and API enforcement must be established. Do not substitute SDK fields for a verified hard cash guarantee.

**I:** Budgets control delivery under provider rules; actual incurred spend, posted invoices, credits, taxes/fees/FX, unsettled contributions, disputes and payment timing determine liabilities and available cash. Reserve against these obligations before committing spend or releasing unused funds. Account caps supplement campaign controls; neither replaces reconciliation or the external provider's balance contract.

| Failure example | Boundary and proposed stop condition (I) |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong account/parent or beneficiary selected | B/C permit allowlists per nonprofit/account; A relies on finer object mapping. All can misroute if the top-level binding is wrong. Stop before enabling whenever account, identity, destination or parent binding disagrees. |
| Wrong Page/TikTok identity or broad grant | Extra accounts help only if unnecessary identities and members are excluded. Stop the affected scope when identity consent or verified access is lost; reauthorize deliberately. |
| Campaign budget increased twice after timeout | Every layout needs read-back and uncertain-write reconciliation. Stop budget increases while the resulting state is unknown; separate accounts do not make retries safe. |
| One campaign exhausts cash or payment fails | A can interrupt every nonprofit in that advertiser; C affects sibling campaigns; B narrows account delivery effects but shared billing/credit can still propagate failure. Stop new commitments when approved available funds after reservations are insufficient or payment authority fails. |
| Reports delayed while costs accrue | Do not treat last reported spend as final or drain a provider balance to zero. Pause new allocations/delivery according to the selected risk policy when reconciliation freshness/completeness is inadequate; no numerical tolerance is invented here. |
| Account restricted or business/app grant revoked | Stop affected writes and reconcile delivery through an authorized operator. Account separation does not guarantee that workspace, app, person, payer, asset or policy restrictions spare other accounts. Never create replacement accounts to evade enforcement. |

## Migration, closure and exit

**D:** TikTok's [September 2026 BC transfer guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-transfer-ad-accounts-in-tiktok-business-center?redirected=2) supersedes older blanket descriptions of transfer impossibility: self-service transfers may be available but require eligible approved accounts/BCs, sufficient recipient quota, compatible currency/payment arrangements and settlement checks. Active campaigns pause. Cross-company balances return to the original payment workspace rather than following the account; same-company handling differs. Linked assets require permissions, and the article warns campaigns using linked assets may need recreation. Do not promise an interruption-free handoff or fund portability.

**D:** [TikTok BC refunds](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/request-a-refund-for-ad-accounts-with-a-business-center) describes a balance-transfer-to-owning-BC alternative and warns that refunding can require closure of all associated ad accounts; managed customers contact their representative. Refunds return to the original method. **I:** per-campaign advertiser accounts in one BC therefore do not guarantee independently redeemable campaign cash.

**U:** Meta ownership/claim permanence, closure quota and migration rules need readable official confirmation. Design the exit contract before choosing Slander ownership. **I:** moving from A/C to B is not assumed to move existing platform campaigns/history/learning. Plan new account objects, explicit old/new mapping, reports retained under original IDs, separately reauthorized identities and reconciled billing cutover. Duplication or re-creation is not historical continuity. Ending one campaign must not close a shared nonprofit account or revoke a grant still used by siblings.

## Recommendation conditions and unresolved checks

- **C, per nonprofit:** preferred planning baseline when several Slander campaigns belong to one nonprofit, one approved account-level billing arrangement can support separately reconciled campaign funds, and campaign-level access/spend controls suffice. This minimizes repeated advertiser onboarding without conflating separate nonprofit entities. With one campaign per nonprofit it initially looks identical to B; their difference appears on the nonprofit's next campaign.
- **B, per Slander campaign:** prefer when approved account-level caps/permissions or an approved one-instrument-per-account payment design materially support the user's campaign separation requirement. First confirm actual creation quota, nonprofit verification reuse, overhead, unused-balance recovery and ownership exit. It remains a conditional design, not proof of independent funds or policy isolation.
- **A, shared across nonprofits:** consider only after obtaining current platform-specific authorization for the exact multi-end-advertiser/identity model, correct disclosures and any exception requirements, plus an approved aggregated billing allocation rail. No such approval was established here. Generic multi-account Business Center capabilities do not approve this option.

Outstanding gates, deliberately preserved after research closure:

1. Readable current Meta account-structure policies/exception criteria; whether any exception actually fits nonprofit fundraising rather than commerce; written support where required. Exact creation quota, ownership/claim/transfer/closure restrictions and spend-cap semantics.
2. TikTok Sales Representative approval for adoption.is's actual nonprofit and markets, creative/destination/fundraising model; acceptable agency versus partner ownership and advertiser declaration. Repeat for later nonprofits rather than assuming adoption.is approval extends to them.
3. Actual approved BC/portfolio types and quotas; legal entity verification, ad-identity verification/consent, owner and payer identities, finance/member grants and app-level production permissions. No live account was inspected.
4. End-to-end approved external provider campaign balance → accepted instrument/settlement rail → correct platform advertiser/billing account. Crowdfunding acceptance, separate-balance meaning, third-party payer permission, accepted card type/currency, cash/credit allocation eligibility and refund/dispute reserves remain unknown. No approved automated funding route is established by this note.
5. Current API endpoint contracts for chosen provisioning, campaign/status/budget, reporting and finance operations: scopes, object ownership validation, version, quota, error/retry semantics and test options. Generated SDK surfaces are insufficient for execution readiness.
6. Human choices in the existing ownership, funding, controls, consent and architecture tickets. Those tickets remain open; research closure supplies evidence and gates, not a decision on their behalf. The parallel fund-separation investigation must also resolve before dependent human choices are considered unblocked.

This bounded investigation is complete with the above explicit unknowns. It makes the three operating paths concrete without authorizing or claiming setup, eligibility, automatic funding, legal segregation or implementation readiness.
